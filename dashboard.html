Daily stock prices, turned into monthly insight

An end-to-end view of my data engineering assignment: what was asked, what I built, how the code is organised, and the real output for each stock. The code and the charts below are read live from the script and result files in this repo.

What the assignment gave me

Input

  • One CSV of daily prices, 2 years, 10 stocks
  • Columns: date, volume, open, high, low, close, adjclose, ticker

Asked for

  • Resample daily data to monthly
  • Open = first trading day, Close = last trading day, High = max, Low = min
  • SMA 10 and 20, EMA 10 and 20, Donchian Channels, Bollinger Bands, Z-score on the monthly close

Rules

  • Python and Pandas only
  • 10 files named result_{SYMBOL}.csv, exactly 24 rows each
  • Modular functions: calculation separate from file writing
  • Assumptions written in the README

What I built and calculated

Monthly rows

Each month keeps four numbers.

  • open: first trading day
  • high: highest of the month
  • low: lowest of the month
  • close: last trading day

Trend indicators

All on the monthly close.

  • SMA 10 / 20: plain average of the last 10 or 20 closes
  • EMA 10 / 20: average that weights recent months more, starting from the SMA

Range indicators

  • Bollinger Bands: 20-month average plus or minus 2 standard deviations
  • Donchian Channel: highest high and lowest low of 20 months
  • Z-score: how far the close is from its 20-month average, in standard deviations

Choices and assumptions

  • Window of 20 months for Bollinger, Donchian and Z-score, since the brief gave none. Bollinger uses k = 2.
  • Sample standard deviation (Pandas default) for Bollinger and Z-score.
  • Empty cells are kept empty. A 10-month average cannot exist before month 10, so early rows stay blank instead of being filled.
  • EMA starts from the SMA, as the reference formula suggests.
  • Strict checks. The script stops if a ticker is missing or a stock does not give exactly 24 months.
  • volume and adjclose are not part of the required output, so they are not carried forward.
